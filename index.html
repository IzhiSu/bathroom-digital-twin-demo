Bathroom_DigitalTwin

Project Purpose and Significance

This project develops a bathroom-centered human–building digital twin for interaction-aware indoor safety analysis. The core purpose is to connect three types of evidence in one interpretable environment: the physical bathroom layout, the dynamic human motion process, and the contact or near-contact events between the body and surrounding fixtures.

Rather than treating the bathroom only as a static 3D model, the project uses the digital twin as a spatial reasoning platform. It supports the analysis of where risky interactions occur, which objects are involved, and how human motion patterns relate to environmental constraints.

Why integrate with a DT?

Digital twin integration allows case materials to become an analyzable environment where human motion, object locations, and contact events can be organized and interpreted together.

Why this project matters

  • From geometry to interaction: The project moves beyond simple environment visualization by linking spatial layout with human motion, body–object contact, and safety-relevant event records.
  • From generic fall detection to spatial interpretation: The system does not only identify that a risky motion may occur; it also helps interpret where it occurs and which bathroom objects may contribute to impact or obstruction.
  • From prototype demonstration to design evidence: The resulting DT environment can support later comparison of bathroom layouts, fixture arrangements, and intervention strategies before physical renovation or deployment.
Project positioning: This is an early-stage research prototype. It should be understood as a framework for human–building interaction representation and indoor safety analysis, not as a finished clinical fall-prediction product or a complete smart-home deployment system.

Key Capabilities

  • Human–building interaction mapping: Maps human posture and motion trajectories against bathroom fixtures and spatial zones.
  • Spatial risk interpretation: Identifies potential impact locations and object-level interaction points, such as washbasin, toilet, bathtub, wall, or floor areas.
  • Kinematic and event tracking: Supports the extraction of motion-related indicators such as body position, velocity, drop process, first-contact timing, and contact sequence.
  • Case-grounded DT construction: Links field case documentation and environment digitization with a reusable digital twin scene for later analysis.

How to Use

Use the on-screen UI buttons to trigger different motion scenarios. The "Complex Scenario" demonstrates a full sequence from walking to a potential fall, while the individual buttons trigger specific motion states.

Development status: This demo represents an early-stage prototype for phase-based demonstration. Advanced kinematic data extraction, physics-based interaction, multi-case comparison, and extended analytical features are currently under development.
